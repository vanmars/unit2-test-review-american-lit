Claim Clinic

Each draft claim has one main problem (or none). Pick what is wrong, then study the supercharged revision.

Analytical claim formula
[Author] uses [literary choice] to [strong verb] [theme].
The theme is the SO WHAT: a universal statement about life, not a one-word topic and not plot summary.
The literary choice is the HOW: the device, tone, or shift that develops the theme.
Supercharge it:
  1. Replace the device with a precise adjective + noun (figurative language → haunting metaphors).
  2. Replace “show” with a stronger analytical verb (reveals, conveys, suggests, illustrates).
  3. Invert the sentence order to try a different structure.
  4. Add a “they say” statement: While some believe [title] is about [x], it actually conveys [theme].

Quote Check

Each sentence tries one of our three integration methods. Find the lead-in or punctuation error before you flip. A few sentences are already correct, so read carefully.

Our three integration methods
1 · Lead-in + commaContext and speaker, then a comma, then the quote.
2 · Embed in actionThe quote flows as part of your sentence: no comma, no capital letter.
3 · ColonA complete sentence of your own, then a colon, then the quote.
  • Embedding + “Quote” + (Citation). The period goes after the citation.
  • Give context before the quote; never drop a quote in alone or start with it.
  • Brackets [ ] mark added or changed words. Ellipses ( . . . ) mark omitted words.
  • Avoid “This quote shows that…”
Citations here show only the author’s last name because page numbers vary by copy. On your essays, use (Lastname #).
